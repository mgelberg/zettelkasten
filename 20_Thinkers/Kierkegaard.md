# Søren Kierkegaard

**Dates**: 1813-1855
**Tradition**: Danish existentialism, Christian philosophy

## Key Ideas

**Anxiety (angst/dread)**: Not fear of a specific thing but the "dizziness of freedom"—the overwhelming awareness that you're radically free to choose, that the future is open, that you could do *anything*.

**The vertigo of possibility**: Standing at a cliff, you fear falling but you're anxious about the fact that you could jump. You must continuously re-choose not to, with no guarantee.

**Decision paralysis**: Reflection has "inner infinity"—you can always think one more step. To actually decide, you have to *will* yourself to stop reflecting and commit. The "leap."

**Either/Or**: The choice between aesthetic life (pleasure, variety, irony) and ethical life (commitment, duty, meaning). You can't have both—you must choose.

**Authenticity vs. the crowd**: "The crowd is untruth." Authentic existence means standing as an individual, making your own choices, not dissolving into conformity.

**Subjectivity is truth**: For matters of faith and existence, objective proof is impossible and irrelevant. What matters is how you live it, the passion of your commitment.

## Major Works
- *Either/Or* (1843)
- *Fear and Trembling* (1843)
- *The Concept of Anxiety* (1844)
- *The Sickness Unto Death* (1849)

## Relevance to Our Discussions
Kierkegaard diagnosed the problem of overthinking before phenomenology existed. His insight that "thinking can go on forever" connects directly to the trap of trying to solve everything before acting. Anxiety about infinite possibility is the condition of freedom.

## Related Thinkers
- [[Heidegger]] - Heavily influenced by Kierkegaard's concepts of anxiety and authenticity
- [[Marcuse]] - Frankfurt School drew on Kierkegaard for understanding alienation and despair
- [[William James]] - Different response to uncertainty: James pragmatic, Kierkegaard existential
