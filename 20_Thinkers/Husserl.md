# Edmund Husserl

**Dates**: 1859-1938
**Tradition**: German phenomenology (founder)

## Key Ideas

**Phenomenology**: "To the things themselves" (*zu den Sachen selbst*). Describe phenomena as they actually appear in experience, before theoretical interpretation.

**Intentionality**: Consciousness is always consciousness *of* something. You can't have contentless thought—awareness is always directed toward an object.

**Phenomenological reduction (epoché)**: "Bracket" assumptions about external reality to focus purely on how things appear in consciousness. Suspend the "natural attitude."

**Eidetic reduction**: Through imaginative variation, discover the essential structures that make a phenomenon what it is. What features can't be removed without destroying the phenomenon?

**Lifeworld (*Lebenswelt*)**: The pre-scientific world of lived experience. Science builds on this but often forgets its foundations.

## Major Works
- *Logical Investigations* (1900-1901)
- *Ideas Pertaining to a Pure Phenomenology* (1913)
- *The Crisis of European Sciences* (1936)

## Relevance to Our Discussions
Husserl founded phenomenology, which [[Heidegger]], [[Merleau-Ponty]], and [[Marcuse]] all built upon (while also departing from). His emphasis on returning to lived experience before theory connects to your interest in "the real, the nameable, the concrete."

## Related Thinkers
- [[Heidegger]] - Student who radicalized phenomenology toward existential questions
- [[Merleau-Ponty]] - Developed Husserl's later work on embodiment and lifeworld
- [[Marcuse]] - Drew on Husserl's lifeworld concept for social critique
- [[William James]] - Husserl incorporated James's psychological insights
