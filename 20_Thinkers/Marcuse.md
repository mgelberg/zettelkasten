# Herbert Marcuse

**Dates**: 1898-1979
**Tradition**: Frankfurt School, Critical Theory, Phenomenological Marxism

## Key Ideas

**One-dimensional society**: Advanced industrial capitalism flattens thought and experience, eliminating the capacity to imagine alternatives. Everything becomes functional, instrumental, measured by utility.

**Performance principle**: The capitalist version of Freud's "reality principle"—life organized around productive labor, efficiency, repression of pleasure. Goes beyond what's necessary for civilization.

**Repressive desublimation**: Modern society releases sexual and instinctual energies, but in controlled, commercialized ways that defuse rebellion. You're "free" to consume sexuality but not to genuinely liberate Eros.

**The Great Refusal**: Rejecting the terms of one-dimensional society entirely. Not reforming the system but refusing to play by its rules.

**Negative thinking**: Dialectical thought that recognizes contradictions and imagines what could be, not just what is. Opposed to positivism and purely affirmative thinking.

**Aesthetic dimension**: Art and beauty reveal possibilities foreclosed by instrumental rationality. Liberation must be sensuous, embodied, aesthetic—not just economic.

## Major Works
- *Eros and Civilization* (1955)
- *One-Dimensional Man* (1964)
- *The Aesthetic Dimension* (1977)

## Relevance to Our Discussions
Marcuse connects phenomenology to revolutionary politics. He takes [[Heidegger]]'s insights about embodied existence and asks: why is sensuous, authentic life so difficult under capitalism? His critique of "total work" parallels [[Pieper]]'s defense of leisure, though from materialist rather than religious foundations.

## Related Thinkers
- [[Heidegger]] - Early influence; Marcuse studied with him but broke over Nazism
- [[Merleau-Ponty]] - Both phenomenological, but Marcuse more explicitly political
- [[Pieper]] - Both critique "total work" culture, but Marcuse is materialist where Pieper is Thomist
- [[Kierkegaard]] - Influenced Frankfurt School's interest in anxiety and decision
