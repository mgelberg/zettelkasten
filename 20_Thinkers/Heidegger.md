# Martin Heidegger

**Dates**: 1889-1976
**Tradition**: German phenomenology, existentialism

## Key Ideas

**Being-in-the-world** (*Dasein*): We're not detached minds observing reality from outside. We're always already embedded in practical, worldly engagement. The world isn't a set of objects we contemplate—it's what we're thrown into.

**Authenticity vs. Inauthenticity**: Most of the time we live according to "the They" (*das Man*)—conforming to social expectations, fleeing from our own finitude. Authentic existence means owning your mortality and making genuine choices.

**Ready-to-hand vs. Present-at-hand**: We encounter tools through use (ready-to-hand) before we theorize about them (present-at-hand). The hammer is graspable before it's an "object with properties."

**Temporality**: Human existence is fundamentally temporal—we're always projecting toward future possibilities while carrying our past. Not stuck in a frozen present.

**Technology as "enframing"**: Modern technology doesn't just make tools—it transforms everything (including nature and humans) into "standing reserve," resources to be optimized and exploited.

## Major Works
- *Being and Time* (1927)
- "The Question Concerning Technology" (1954)

## Relevance to Our Discussions
Heidegger's phenomenology is the foundation for both [[Merleau-Ponty]] and [[Marcuse]], though they take it in very different directions. His critique of technological rationality influenced [[Marcuse]]'s analysis of one-dimensional society. His emphasis on embodied, practical engagement anticipates [[Merleau-Ponty]]'s focus on perception.

## Related Thinkers
- [[Merleau-Ponty]] - Developed Heidegger's phenomenology toward embodied perception
- [[Marcuse]] - Radicalized Heidegger politically, though broke with him over Nazism
- [[Kierkegaard]] - Major influence on Heidegger's concepts of anxiety and authenticity
- [[Husserl]] - Heidegger's teacher, founder of phenomenology
