# Maurice Merleau-Ponty

**Dates**: 1908-1961
**Tradition**: French phenomenology

## Key Ideas

**Embodied perception**: The body isn't an object we have—it's our way of being-in-the-world. Perception happens through the lived body, not through a disembodied mind processing sense data.

**Body schema**: Pre-reflective awareness of our body's position and capacities. Your hand "knows" how to reach for a glass before conscious thought kicks in.

**Primacy of perception**: Before scientific analysis or intellectual reflection, we're already perceptually engaged with the world. This pre-reflective experience is foundational.

**Phenomenological description over explanation**: Stay with how things actually appear in lived experience rather than rushing to causal explanations or abstract theories.

**The visible and the invisible**: Later work on how perception reveals both what's present and what's absent, the seen and the unseen intertwined.

## Major Works
- *Phenomenology of Perception* (1945)
- *The Visible and the Invisible* (1964, posthumous)

## Relevance to Our Discussions
Merleau-Ponty provides philosophical grounding for the idea that you can't be "just in your head"—consciousness is always embodied, always worldly. His work supports staying with concrete, lived experience rather than abstract analysis.

Contrasts with [[Marcuse]]: Merleau-Ponty stays more descriptive where Marcuse turns explicitly political. Both draw from [[Heidegger]] but diverge significantly.

## Related Thinkers
- [[Heidegger]] - Major influence, especially *Being and Time*
- [[Husserl]] - Merleau-Ponty develops Husserl's phenomenology in new directions
- [[Marcuse]] - Both phenomenological, but Marcuse more explicitly revolutionary
- [[Pieper]] - Both value receptive understanding, though Merleau-Ponty doesn't ground it religiously
