# William James

**Dates**: 1842-1910
**Tradition**: American pragmatism, psychology

## Key Ideas

**Pragmatism**: Truth is what works in practice. An idea's meaning lies in its practical consequences. Don't ask "Is it absolutely true?" but "What difference does it make if I believe this?"

**Stream of consciousness**: Thought flows continuously, not as discrete units. You can't freeze experience into static moments. Influenced modernist literature.

**Radical empiricism**: Experience includes relations, transitions, feelings—not just discrete "things." The connections between experiences are as real as the experiences themselves.

**The will to believe**: When evidence is inconclusive but decision is necessary, you're entitled to choose based on what will work best for you. Faith is justified if it makes life better.

**Pluralism**: Reality is open, multiple, unfinished. Reject "block universe" thinking where everything is one closed system. Keep possibilities open.

**Varieties of religious experience**: Studied mystical and spiritual experiences empirically. If religious belief works for someone, that's evidence *for them*.

## Major Works
- *The Principles of Psychology* (1890)
- *The Varieties of Religious Experience* (1902)
- *Pragmatism* (1907)
- *A Pluralistic Universe* (1909)

## Relevance to Our Discussions
James offers an action-oriented alternative to contemplation. Where [[Pieper]] values receptive stillness, James values experimental doing. His pragmatism could support your focus on "the real, the concrete" but might miss the non-instrumental dimension [[Pieper]] defends. Useful corrective to overthinking: just try something and see if it works.

## Related Thinkers
- [[Kierkegaard]] - Both address decision under uncertainty, but James more optimistic/experimental
- [[Pieper]] - Direct opposition: James action-oriented, Pieper contemplation-oriented
- [[Heidegger]] - James influenced phenomenology, though he's less systematic
- [[Husserl]] - Incorporated James's psychological insights into phenomenology
