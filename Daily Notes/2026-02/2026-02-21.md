Trying to maintain the habit, ya know. Fucking exhausted. The people upstairs at the AirBnb seem to never sleep, I swear they were walking around from 12-3 (and my sleep app would seem to confirm that).

 But let's look at the bright side too. I'm sitting outside, without a jacket (though that may have been a slight mistake). Having an americano on 4th street, in the neighborhood I live. There's kids and people walking in the streets. The sun's doing what it can before another round of snow comes in.

We got to see the cabinetry yesterday, went to Andre's workshop out in East Falls. It made the [[Kitchen Build]] feel a lot more real. It's to going to look so fucking good. We came back to the house as well to talk tile stuff. It felt like we had a lot of control over things, certainly more than before the doors were in place. Making snap judgments on things like the pesky overhead lights, just nixing them, with no friction. One of the positives of homeownership, ya know?

So hopefully this americano keeps me going. Planning to go do a bit of a neighborhood bar crawl, though it could also be limited to Southwark. Would like to start a new book, maybe that one about the revolutionary groups of the 70s.

I finished Altered States, it kind of fit what I was going for in terms of a detective noir but sci fi, but I didn't like the fact that he was a superhuman, essentially. I need my detectives to be a bit more of a fuck up. I think there were times it was almost hard to imagine a world that really *wanted* that potentially limitless life. That people would be totally comfortable with winding up in different people's bodies interchangeably. I think the meths were quite resonant though. Not that there are people who are 300 years old or whatever. But as far as being associated with [[transhumanism]] and feeling above the rest of society.

I guess I haven't written about it as much here, but I've definitely binged a lot of Epstein content over the past couple of months. And honestly I'm not going to articulate it nearly as well as others already have, particularly Anand's essay in The Ink. But it's important to recognize that these aren't just scifi fantasies. There are real people with a disdain, or maybe even a discomfort, with normal human life. With the messiness of it all. It's apparent in the worst parts of AI as well, as far as taking humans out of the loop. Having an AI friend and so forth. We need to fight for humanity. Fight for the messy. Fight for the differences.

I don't know, maybe I'll try to write on it more. I want us to have something real to bind us all together. This AI stuff is really getting to be dramatic, we need to amplify the humanness of everything now, and what matters most to us, before it all goes down the shitter.
