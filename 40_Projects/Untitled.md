# TouchTunes: the bar entertainment platform preparing for its next act

**TouchTunes is North America's largest connected in-venue entertainment platform, operating ~95,000 jukeboxes and electronic dartboards across bars, restaurants, and social venues.** Backed by TA Associates since March 2022, the company is in the middle of a strategic transformation—from a digital jukebox operator into a unified social entertainment ecosystem spanning music, darts, mobile payments, and advertising. With a new CEO, a redesigned mobile app, and a freshly unveiled product lineup (March 2026), TouchTunes is betting that the future of bar entertainment is connected, cashless, and social. For anyone interviewing for an FP&A or analytics role there, understanding the unit economics of a distributed hardware-and-software platform—and the growth levers TA Associates is pulling—is essential.

---

## How TouchTunes actually makes money

TouchTunes operates a **three-sided marketplace** connecting the company, ~2,500 independent amusement operators, and 63,000+ bar and restaurant venues. The business model works like this: operators purchase jukebox hardware from TouchTunes and install it in venues **at no cost to the venue**. Revenue from patron song plays is then split three ways—roughly **25–30% to TouchTunes, ~40% to the operator, and ~30–40% to the venue**. Exact splits vary by contract and are not publicly disclosed.

The company's revenue streams break into five categories. **Music service revenue** is the core: patrons buy credits (~$0.50 each) via the mobile app or on-jukebox payment, then spend them on song plays (~$1/song) or premium features like Fast Pass queue-skipping (~$1.50). A productive jukebox location can gross **$500–$600 per week**, adding $7,000+ in annual revenue for a venue. **Advertising revenue** comes from TouchTunes Media, their digital out-of-home (DOOH) platform that sells screen time on jukebox displays and in-app placements to brands like White Claw, Jack Daniel's, Captain Morgan, and Universal Pictures. They claim **38 million unique monthly venue visitors** and a **50% click-through rate**on mobile ads. The company notably retains 100% of alcohol-category advertising revenue—no venue share. **Hardware sales** to operators generate upfront revenue on each jukebox (historically in the $4,000–$5,500 range) and now on BullShooter dartboards following the Arachnid acquisition. **Mobile app monetization** through in-app credit purchases has become increasingly significant—over **30% of UK revenue** now comes through the app, and the company reported a "substantial increase in app-generated revenue" in early 2025. Finally, the emerging **TouchTunes Pay/FunWallet**cashless payment platform aims to process transactions across all in-venue amusement machines, not just TouchTunes' own devices.

Third-party estimates place total revenue at approximately **$100–$115 million annually**, though as a private company TouchTunes does not disclose financials.

## TA Associates bought a platform, not just a jukebox company

TA Associates completed its acquisition of TouchTunes on **March 30, 2022**, purchasing the company from Searchlight Capital Partners and Newlight Partners. Financial terms were not disclosed, though TA's typical deal range involves **$100–$600 million in equity** for businesses valued at **$150 million to over $3 billion**. LionTree Advisors served as financial advisor; Debevoise & Plimpton provided legal counsel to TA.

TA Associates manages **$65 billion in cumulative capital** and focuses on profitable, growing companies in technology, healthcare, financial services, consumer, and business services. Their value-creation playbook centers on three pillars: **growth acceleration** (portfolio companies typically grow 15–30%), **bolt-on acquisitions** to build platforms, and **operational transformation**. Michael Berk, the TA Managing Director who led the deal, stated the firm sees "opportunity for continued innovation and expansion"—code for TA's well-known strategy of using a market-leading asset as a platform for add-on acquisitions and new market entry.

The post-acquisition moves confirm this playbook precisely. Within two years, TA orchestrated the **acquisition of Arachnid 360** (February 2024), the dominant soft-tip electronic darts company with 30,000+ connected BullShooter dartboards and an estimated **~85% market share** in electronic darts. This bolt-on instantly expanded TouchTunes' connected device footprint to nearly 100,000 units and opened an entirely new entertainment vertical with cross-sell potential across the existing venue base. They also launched FunWallet (cashless payments), partnered with Lucra (real-money gaming), and invested heavily in redesigning the mobile app—all hallmarks of TA's approach to accelerating digital transformation and building recurring revenue streams.

**TouchTunes' ownership history** tells a story of progressive PE-driven professionalization: early institutional investors (3i Group, VantagePoint Capital) gave way to Searchlight Capital's control acquisition in 2015 (reportedly **$57 million**), which then executed the PlayNetwork merger (2017) and subsequent divestiture to Mood Media (2021), before TA Associates stepped in as the growth-phase owner.

## The competitive landscape and where the risks live

TouchTunes dominates its niche—**65,000+ jukeboxes versus AMI Entertainment's ~30,000**—but faces genuine strategic challenges. The global digital jukebox market was valued at **$382 million in 2024** and is projected to reach **$812 million by 2033** (8.7% CAGR), but growth is not guaranteed for any single player.

**Streaming services pose an indirect but real threat.** Many venues simply play Spotify or Apple Music through house speakers—technically a licensing violation for commercial use, but enforcement by performing rights organizations (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, GMR) is inconsistent. TouchTunes' counter is that it bundles all music licensing costs (saving venues $1,000–$3,000+ annually in PRO fees) and that the social experience of controlling a bar's music is fundamentally different from passive streaming. The data supports this: patrons spend an average **35 minutes engaged with TouchTunes per visit** and venue visits average **1.5+ hours**.

**Advertising monetization is promising but early-stage.** The broader DOOH advertising market is growing at **~10–12% CAGR** (projected $32–56 billion globally by 2030–2034), and TouchTunes claims to be the largest DOOH network by gross monthly adult impressions. However, OOH advertising's share of total U.S. media spend has actually slipped from **3.1% in 2022 to 2.7% in 2025** as digital channels absorb more budgets. Programmatic DOOH—where TouchTunes has integrations with Magnite and Place Exchange—remains nascent, and convincing media buyers to treat jukebox screens as a serious advertising channel requires continued investment in measurement infrastructure (they partnered with Epicenter Experience in 2021 for in-venue audience measurement).

**The darts expansion has strong logic but execution risks.** Cross-selling 30,000+ dartboards into 63,000+ jukebox venues (and vice versa) is a clear growth lever. The Lucra real-money gaming integration could materially boost revenue per dartboard. But regulatory complexity around state-by-state gambling laws, the niche size of electronic darts, and the integration challenges of combining two technology platforms all merit careful modeling.

## March 2026 marks a strategic inflection point

TouchTunes' most significant product announcements came just days ago. At **Amusement Expo 2026 (March 17, 2026)**, the company unveiled what CEO Scott Levine called "a new era for in-venue social entertainment":

- **A new flagship jukebox** with a 43-inch vertical display, AI-driven music discovery software, proximity-triggered "wake up" feature, customizable immersive lighting, and contactless tap-to-pay—designed to serve as both entertainment hub and future social feed/leaderboard display
- **A compact 15-inch jukebox** targeting new venue types like coffee shops and pizzerias, expanding the addressable market beyond traditional bars
- **TouchTunes Pay** now live on **11,000+ BullShooter dartboards**, creating a unified cashless payment system across the full device ecosystem
- **A unified amusements platform** connecting music, darts, mobile, and payments into a single ecosystem—the culmination of TA Associates' platform-building strategy

The company also officially shifted its positioning language from "interactive music and entertainment platform" to **"North America's largest connected platform for in-venue social entertainment."** This rebrand reflects the strategic bet that the future value lies in the connected platform—not in any single hardware device.

**Leadership has turned over significantly under TA ownership.** Scott Levine became CEO in May 2025, replacing Ross Honey (who departed January 2025 after nine years). Levine's background—CPO at Brightcove, senior roles at Univision and AOL—signals a bet on digital product and platform expertise. Charles Goldstuck, the long-tenured former CEO, serves as Executive Chairman. Other key hires include Angela Abbott as VP of Music Licensing & Strategy (January 2026, formerly at Pandora and TIDAL) and Suresh Somasundaram as CTO (formerly SVP Engineering at Brightcove).

## Financial modeling frameworks for an FP&A interview

For a VP of Financial Planning or Director of Analytics interview at TouchTunes, expect questions centered on how you'd model a distributed hardware-platform business with multiple revenue streams. Here are the key frameworks and how they apply specifically to TouchTunes.

**Unit economics per device** is the foundation. For each jukebox or dartboard, you'd model: ==average weekly revenue per device== (gross), TouchTunes' share of that revenue after operator and venue splits, variable costs per device (content licensing fees, connectivity/cellular costs, payment processing), and the resulting **contribution margin per device per month**. The key driver is utilization—a jukebox in a busy sports bar might gross $600/week while one in a quiet restaurant grosses $100/week. Segmenting devices into performance tiers (A/B/C locations) and modeling the revenue distribution curve would demonstrate analytical sophistication. A strong candidate would note that the **marginal cost of an additional song play is nearly zero** (the music licensing is a fixed/stepped cost, not per-play), so contribution margin improves dramatically with utilization. For dartboards, unit economics include per-game revenue, tournament fees, and the incremental value of FunWallet/Lucra transactions.

**Venue partner lifetime value (LTV)** captures the total economic value of a venue relationship. To model this, you need: average monthly net revenue per venue (across all streams—music plays, advertising impressions, app-driven revenue), the expected venue relationship duration (likely measured in years given the sticky, no-cost-to-venue model), and a discount rate. The formula is straightforward: LTV = (Monthly Net Revenue × Gross Margin) / Monthly Churn Rate. What makes this interesting at TouchTunes is that LTV should be modeled as **increasing over time** as the company layers additional revenue streams (advertising, darts, TouchTunes Pay) onto the same venue relationship. A sophisticated LTV model would include cohort-based revenue ramps showing how venues generate more revenue in year 3 than year 1 as the app penetrates the patron base and new products are cross-sold.

**Churn modeling for venue partners** is critical because the entire business rests on maintaining the installed base. Venue churn could be driven by: venue closure (bars have high failure rates—roughly 60% close within the first year, 80% within five years), operator switching (a venue's operator might swap to an AMI jukebox), or underperformance (if a jukebox doesn't generate meaningful revenue, the operator may relocate it). You'd model churn by building a **survival curve** segmented by venue type, geography, and device performance. ==Leading indicators of churn might include declining weekly plays, reduced app check-ins at a venue, or falling below a minimum revenue threshold.== Net retention rate (accounting for expansion revenue from existing venues through new products) would be the headline metric—and TouchTunes should be targeting **net retention above 100%** as they cross-sell darts and payments.

**Advertising revenue forecasting** requires a bottoms-up model. Start with: number of ad-enabled screens (63,000+ jukeboxes plus growing mobile app inventory), average daily operating hours per venue (~10–14 hours), available ad impressions per hour (based on the attract loop frequency—how often the jukebox shows ads versus music content), fill rate (percentage of available impressions actually sold), and effective CPM (revenue per thousand impressions). Total ad revenue = Screens × Daily Hours × Impressions/Hour × Fill Rate × CPM / 1,000 × 365. The growth levers are fill rate improvement (through programmatic integrations with Magnite/Place Exchange), CPM increases (through better measurement and targeting), and inventory expansion (more screens, more venue types). A strong interview answer would note the **chicken-and-egg problem**: ==advertisers want proven measurement before committing budgets, but measurement investment requires advertiser demand to justify. ==TouchTunes' ==Epicenter Experience== partnership is their bet on solving this.

**Cohort analysis for mobile app users** would track: acquisition cohort (month a user first downloaded/used the app), ==retention curves (what percentage are still active at 30/60/90/180/360 days),== revenue per user over time (do users spend more per session as they mature?), and the impact of new features on reactivation. With **2 million+ monthly active users**and reported 56% user base growth after adding Apple/Google Pay, the app is clearly a strategic priority. A key metric is **app-to-play conversion rate**: what percentage of app users in a venue actually spend credits versus just browsing? Another is **cross-product engagement**: do users who interact with both music and darts features have higher LTV? Given the March 2025 app relaunch with social features, expect questions about how you'd measure the impact of Social Reactions on retention and monetization using A/B testing or interrupted time-series analysis.

**Pricing optimization** at TouchTunes is a rich analytical problem. The credit-based system already supports **dynamic pricing**: operators can set different credit costs per play by time of day, day of week, and demand level (surge pricing when venues are busy). A pricing optimization model would analyze: price elasticity of demand (how does play volume change as credit cost increases?), optimal surge pricing thresholds (what's the revenue-maximizing price during peak hours versus the risk of pricing out patrons?), and the Fast Pass premium (is the current ~50% premium over standard plays optimal?). The model should consider that **price sensitivity likely varies enormously by venue type**—a dive bar patron may be more price-sensitive than someone at an upscale sports bar. Geographic variation matters too. Demonstrated familiarity with concepts like conjoint analysis, ==A/B price testing==, and two-part tariff structures (fixed credit purchase + variable per-play cost) would resonate.

**Contribution margin by product line** is the CFO-level view. You'd want to model separate P&Ls for: music (jukebox plays), advertising, darts, mobile app/payments, and hardware sales. Each line has very different margin profiles. Music service revenue has **high incremental margins** (fixed licensing costs, near-zero marginal cost per play) but is mature. Advertising is **extremely high margin** once sold (no COGS per impression) but has significant sales and measurement overhead. Hardware sales likely carry **lower margins** (physical manufacturing, logistics, installation support) but generate the installed base that enables everything else. Darts is a **new vertical with its own cost structure** still being optimized. Showing you can think about the portfolio this way—and identify which product lines deserve incremental investment based on marginal return on capital—is exactly what a VP of FP&A would want to hear.

## Conclusion: what to take into an interview

TouchTunes sits at an unusual intersection: a PE-backed hardware business undergoing a software platform transformation, with a new CEO from the digital media world, an activist owner in TA Associates, and a March 2026 product lineup that signals genuine ambition to become the operating system for bar entertainment. The company's **~100,000 connected devices represent a formidable distribution moat**—it would take a competitor years and hundreds of millions of dollars to replicate this footprint.

The financial story is about **revenue diversification and margin expansion**. TA Associates bought a jukebox company generating ~$100–115M in revenue with a dominant market position but limited growth vectors. They're building an entertainment platform with five distinct revenue streams (music, advertising, darts, payments, hardware), each with different growth trajectories and margin profiles. The key analytical question for any FP&A candidate is: which of these revenue streams has the highest incremental ROI, and how do you model the interconnection effects (e.g., does adding darts to a jukebox venue increase jukebox revenue, and by how much)?

Expect interview conversations to focus on how you'd build financial models for a business where **the installed base is the strategic asset**, revenue per device is the key driver, and the growth thesis rests on layering new monetization onto an existing distribution network. Frame your answers around unit economics, cohort behavior, and portfolio margin optimization—and demonstrate that you understand both the analytical rigor and the strategic context of a PE-backed platform play.
